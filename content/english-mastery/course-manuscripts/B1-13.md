## Understand the situation

Formal & Informal Communication
เป้าหมาย: ปรับระดับความเป็นทางการโดยคงคำขอและข้อเท็จจริง

Use the language of formal & informal communication to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
I need to change the date of a meeting. To a close friend, I might write, 'Sorry, can we move Friday's chat to Monday?' To a new project partner, I would give more context: 'I am writing to ask whether we could reschedule our meeting. I am unavailable on Friday afternoon. Would Monday at ten be convenient?' The facts are the same, but the relationship and purpose affect the language. Formal does not have to mean complicated. A clear subject line, a polite request and a proposed next step are more useful than long sentences that hide the point.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Identify the purpose, relevant constraints and sequence. Distinguish an observation from an explanation. Plan a connected response with reasons, an example and a clear next step. State what remains uncertain.

Reading B
Reply from a project partner: Thank you for letting me know. Monday at ten is not possible, but I am available at eleven. Please confirm whether that works for you. Kind regards, Alex. The meeting is not confirmed until the other person replies.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Can I write 'Hey' to a person I've never met?
B: It depends on the setting, but a neutral greeting is safer for this enquiry.
A: Should I use longer words?
B: Use clear words. Politeness and clarity matter more than length.

Listening script B
Rewrite the same request for two audiences. Keep dates, times and responsibilities unchanged. Compare the greeting, request form and closing. Explain why you changed each feature instead of assuming one style is always better.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use thought groups to make a connected account easy to follow. Stress contrasts and important dates. Repair a sentence calmly when needed; a useful repair is stronger than continuing with a misleading claim.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.

Planning sequence
1. Identify audience and purpose.
2. Select source details that the audience needs.
3. Mark any uncertainty, unconfirmed arrangement or missing information.
4. Choose two language features from Lesson 2.
5. Produce the response, then check meaning before surface accuracy.

Mediation: explain the essential information to a Thai-speaking classmate who has not read the sources. Preserve dates, quantities, conditions, agency and uncertainty. Say what cannot be concluded.

Online interaction: respond to a request for clarification, then add a follow-up checking question. Acknowledge corrections and revise your message where needed.
