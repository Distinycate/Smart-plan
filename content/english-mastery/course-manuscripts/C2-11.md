## Understand the situation

Specialist & Senior Professional Communication
เป้าหมาย: จัดทำข้อความระดับผู้บริหารที่สั้นแต่ไม่ซ่อนความเสี่ยงหรือขอบเขตอำนาจ

Use the language of specialist & senior professional communication to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
A senior briefing on a fictional archive migration must support a decision without burying uncertainty in an appendix. The technical team has completed file transfer, but integrity checks cover only a sample. Describing the migration as complete collapses a delivery milestone into an assurance claim. The briefing should identify the decision requested, the evidence available, the residual uncertainty and the authority responsible for accepting it. Concision is achieved by prioritising decision-relevant distinctions, not by removing every caveat. A recommendation can be firm about the next action while remaining provisional about the system's readiness, provided the conditions for reconsideration are explicit.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Reconstruct the logic and rhetorical purpose of competing accounts. Test presuppositions, compression losses and shifts of agency. Preserve principled disagreement while producing a precise, audience-sensitive synthesis. Consider more than one defensible interpretation and explain why you prefer one.

Reading B
Decision note: Transfer finished. Sample integrity checks passed. Full verification outstanding. Technical lead recommends retaining the old system until verification is complete. Only the programme board can authorise retirement. No evidence in this exercise quantifies the probability of undetected errors.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: The executive wants a yes-or-no answer on retirement.
B: Then recommend no retirement yet, and state the condition that would change the recommendation.
A: Can we say the new system is unsafe?
B: We can say full verification is outstanding. Do not substitute an unsupported risk estimate.

Listening script B
Lead with the action and its rationale. Identify what has been achieved, what remains unverified and who owns the decision. If a specialist term is essential, define it once. Escalate an unresolved decision to the proper authority; do not conceal it inside a reassuring adjective.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prosody strategically to manage complex interaction, signal parenthetical qualification and distinguish reported from personal stance. Judge communicative effect and intelligibility, not resemblance to an idealised native accent.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
