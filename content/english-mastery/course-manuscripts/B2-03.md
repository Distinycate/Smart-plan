## Understand the situation

Media & Critical Interpretation
เป้าหมาย: ตรวจการวางกรอบข่าวและแยกข้อเท็จจริงจากน้ำเสียง

Use the language of media & critical interpretation to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
Two fictional headlines describe the same council proposal. One says, 'Town Finally Reclaims Space from Cars'; the other says, 'Drivers Face Another Restriction.' The proposal would close one street to private cars on two Sunday mornings as a trial. Neither headline is a neutral list of details. Finally implies that change is overdue, while another suggests a continuing burden. The wording may guide readers towards different reactions before they learn the scope of the proposal. A critical response can identify this framing without assuming that either writer is dishonest. The next step is to compare the actual proposal, affected groups and unresolved practical questions.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Map claim, supporting evidence, alternative explanation and limitation. Compare sources on the same question. Build a proportionate conclusion and address a fair counterargument. Explain why your evidence supports a claim rather than merely repeating it.

Reading B
Proposal extract: Trial on the first two Sundays in June, 08:00–12:00. Emergency access retained. Delivery arrangements under consultation. No permanent closure has been approved. All names and policy details are invented for this lesson.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: The headline says cars are being banned from town.
B: The proposal is narrower: one street for two mornings.
A: Does that mean there are no problems?

Listening script B
Notice loaded words, omitted conditions and implied histories. Then separate interpretation from textual evidence. Explain how a reasonable reader might respond differently to each headline, and identify which missing details would matter for evaluating the proposal.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prominence to distinguish your position from a reported view. Pause at changes of argument. Signal concession and contrast with phrasing, without relying on exaggerated stress to make weak evidence sound stronger.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
