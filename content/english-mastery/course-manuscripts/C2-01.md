## Understand the situation

Fine-Grained Precision & Semantic Choice
เป้าหมาย: เลือกคำที่รักษาความต่างละเอียดระหว่างหลักฐาน สิทธิ และข้อผูกพัน

Use the language of fine-grained precision & semantic choice to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
The fictional archive's board described access as guaranteed, although its policy merely made visitors eligible to apply. Eligibility establishes a threshold; a guarantee implies an outcome. Replacing one with the other is therefore a substantive change, not a stylistic improvement. The policy also permitted staff to waive a fee in exceptional circumstances. A summary saying that staff were obliged to do so silently converted discretion into a duty. Precision requires attention to entailment: what must be true if a sentence is true, and what remains merely possible? An editor can make wording more accessible while preserving these distinctions, but cannot resolve an inconvenient ambiguity by inventing a commitment.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Reconstruct the logic and rhetorical purpose of competing accounts. Test presuppositions, compression losses and shifts of agency. Preserve principled disagreement while producing a precise, audience-sensitive synthesis. Consider more than one defensible interpretation and explain why you prefer one.

Reading B
Policy extract: Applicants who meet the residency criterion may request access. Approval remains subject to capacity and preservation requirements. Staff may waive the fee where documented hardship makes payment impracticable. The extract specifies neither an automatic entitlement nor a deadline for decisions.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Could we promise access to every eligible applicant?
B: Only by changing the policy, not by paraphrasing it.
A: Then our heading is misleading.
B: Yes. State who can apply, retain the approval conditions and distinguish a possible waiver from a mandatory one.

Listening script B
Read the revised sentence backwards against the source. Does it now imply an outcome where the original offered a process? Has permission become obligation? A smoother sentence can be less faithful. Where the source is indeterminate, identify the question requiring a policy decision rather than answering it editorially.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prosody strategically to manage complex interaction, signal parenthetical qualification and distinguish reported from personal stance. Judge communicative effect and intelligibility, not resemblance to an idealised native accent.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
