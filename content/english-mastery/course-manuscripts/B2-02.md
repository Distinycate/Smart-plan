## Understand the situation

Extended & Academic Reading Strategies
เป้าหมาย: อ่านงานวิชาการโดยแยกใจความหลัก บทบาทย่อหน้า และข้อจำกัด

Use the language of extended & academic reading strategies to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
A fictional research summary examines how students use a study room. Its opening paragraph describes a practical problem: seats are often empty in the morning but scarce after lunch. The second paragraph explains a four-week observation method. The results section reports occupancy, while the discussion considers possible explanations. The authors suggest that timetable patterns may contribute to the imbalance, but they did not interview students. A reader who skips directly to the recommendation may miss that limitation. Reading strategically means locating the question, method and findings before deciding how strongly the conclusion is supported. A heading is a navigation aid, not a substitute for checking the argument beneath it.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Map claim, supporting evidence, alternative explanation and limitation. Compare sources on the same question. Build a proportionate conclusion and address a fair counterargument. Explain why your evidence supports a claim rather than merely repeating it.

Reading B
Abstract excerpt: Observers counted occupied seats at three fixed times on twenty weekdays. Afternoon occupancy was higher. The study did not record the purpose of visits or students who left because no seat was available. The authors propose further investigation before changing opening hours.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Can I read the abstract instead of the whole report?
B: It helps you judge relevance, but it compresses the method and limitations.

Listening script B
On a first pass, map the sections and identify the central question. On a second pass, trace one conclusion back to its evidence. Mark unfamiliar terms that affect meaning. You do not need to translate every word before understanding the structure.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prominence to distinguish your position from a reported view. Pause at changes of argument. Signal concession and contrast with phrasing, without relying on exaggerated stress to make weak evidence sound stronger.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.

Prepare a 180–240-word response and a 120–180-second spoken response using the unit inputs. Develop sustained positions, evaluate competing practical options and relay complex information with clear qualification.

Planning sequence
1. Identify audience and purpose.
2. Select source details that the audience needs.
3. Mark any uncertainty, unconfirmed arrangement or missing information.
4. Choose two language features from Lesson 2.
5. Produce the response, then check meaning before surface accuracy.

Mediation: explain the essential information to a Thai-speaking classmate who has not read the sources. Preserve dates, quantities, conditions, agency and uncertainty. Say what cannot be concluded.

Online interaction: respond to a request for clarification, then add a follow-up checking question. Acknowledge corrections and revise your message where needed.
