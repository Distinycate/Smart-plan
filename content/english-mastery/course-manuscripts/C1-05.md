## Understand the situation

Register, Style & Tone
เป้าหมาย: ปรับน้ำเสียงและระดับภาษาโดยคงความรับผิดชอบของข้อความ

Use the language of register, style & tone to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
An internal note says, 'We missed the deadline because two approvals arrived late.' A public update changes this to, 'The timetable has been adjusted.' The revised sentence is smoother but removes both the missed commitment and the stated reason. Register adaptation should not become a way to obscure accountability. A more appropriate public version might say, 'We were unable to meet the original date while awaiting two approvals; the revised schedule is below.' The wording remains professional without pretending that the delay was simply a planned adjustment. Tone, detail and directness can change across audiences, but material facts and commitments should remain recoverable.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Track stance, scope and evidential status across sources. Distinguish concession from endorsement and implication from explicit assertion. Synthesize by issue, retaining unresolved disagreement. Adapt density and register to the reader without changing commitments.

Reading B
Audience briefs: Team members need the operational cause and next owner. Service users need the practical effect, revised date and route for questions. A funding body may need the reason, mitigation and reporting implications. None of these audiences benefits from a reassuring phrase that hides a relevant limitation.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

B: Yes, but not by removing the missed deadline.

Listening script B
Before rewriting, identify the reader's purpose and the writer's obligations. Adjust technical detail, courtesy and sentence density. Then compare the versions for changes in agency, certainty, responsibility or promised action. Politeness does not require vagueness.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Shape long turns into coherent rhetorical units. Use prominence and pauses to mark qualification, reformulation and source shifts. Intonation can suggest stance but is not conclusive evidence of intention by itself.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
