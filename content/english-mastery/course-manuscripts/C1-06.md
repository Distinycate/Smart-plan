## Understand the situation

Advanced Inference in Reading
เป้าหมาย: อนุมานจากโครงสร้างและสิ่งที่ละไว้โดยแยกจากข้อกล่าวชัด

Use the language of advanced inference in reading to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
A fictional museum director writes, 'We welcome the proposal's ambition. Before discussing implementation, however, we would need a clearer account of maintenance costs.' The first sentence is positive, but the second establishes a condition that limits immediate support. A reader may reasonably infer caution, yet it would be inaccurate to report that the director has rejected the proposal. The sequence of praise and reservation performs a diplomatic function. Advanced reading requires attention to what a text foregrounds, postpones or leaves unsaid, while resisting the temptation to convert every implication into a definite hidden message. Interpretations should remain tied to specific textual cues and open to alternatives.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Track stance, scope and evidential status across sources. Distinguish concession from endorsement and implication from explicit assertion. Synthesize by issue, retaining unresolved disagreement. Adapt density and register to the reader without changing commitments.

Reading B
Follow-up note: The director requests a revised cost model and has not scheduled an implementation meeting. The note does not say whether a satisfactory model would guarantee approval. The absence of a meeting is evidence of the present stage, not proof of permanent opposition.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Is the director against the proposal?
B: The text shows a reservation about costs. It does not settle her final position.
A: What supports that reading?
B: Before discussing implementation and would need both make further information a condition.

Listening script B
For each inference, cite the words or structural choice that supports it. Then state an alternative interpretation and what further context would help. Do not confuse your knowledge of similar situations with evidence that this particular speaker has the same intention.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Shape long turns into coherent rhetorical units. Use prominence and pauses to mark qualification, reformulation and source shifts. Intonation can suggest stance but is not conclusive evidence of intention by itself.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
