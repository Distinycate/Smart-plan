## Understand the situation

Evaluating Arguments
เป้าหมาย: ประเมินโครงสร้างเหตุผลและค้นหาข้อสมมติที่เชื่อมหลักฐาน

Use the language of evaluating arguments to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
An argument for extending a mentoring programme states that participants reported greater confidence, therefore the programme should be offered to every new employee. The evidence may support continued investigation, but the recommendation depends on additional assumptions: that the sample is representative, confidence predicts relevant outcomes and the programme can scale without losing quality. These assumptions are not necessarily false. They are simply not established by the reported confidence measure alone. Evaluating an argument means reconstructing its strongest defensible form before testing the links. Dismissing it as useless because it is incomplete would be as unhelpful as treating the recommendation as proven.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Track stance, scope and evidential status across sources. Distinguish concession from endorsement and implication from explicit assertion. Synthesize by issue, retaining unresolved disagreement. Adapt density and register to the reader without changing commitments.

Reading B
Counterposition: Expansion should wait until workload and access are examined. This position does not deny that participants valued the programme. It questions whether immediate universal provision is justified. A fair response should address that timing and scope concern rather than accuse critics of opposing support for new staff.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: The confidence results are positive, so why hesitate?
B: Because the proposed scale is much broader than the evidence.
A: What would strengthen the recommendation?
B: Information about non-participants, delivery capacity and outcomes beyond self-reported confidence.

Listening script B
Identify the conclusion, reasons and unstated bridge between them. Test the bridge with a plausible counterexample. Then decide whether the argument should be rejected, narrowed or supported conditionally. Evaluation should improve the reasoning, not merely label a fallacy.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Shape long turns into coherent rhetorical units. Use prominence and pauses to mark qualification, reformulation and source shifts. Intonation can suggest stance but is not conclusive evidence of intention by itself.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
