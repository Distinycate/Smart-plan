## Understand the situation

Fast, Dense & Implicit Listening
เป้าหมาย: ติดตามบทพูดหนาแน่นที่แก้ไขกลางประโยคและแยกข้อยุติจากข้อเสนอ

Use the language of fast, dense & implicit listening to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
The transcript of a fictional planning call contains false starts, embedded qualifications and a late correction. An initial reference to Friday is superseded by Monday, while a proposed launch remains conditional on accessibility testing. A listener who records only prominent nouns and dates may produce a fluent but inaccurate summary. Skilled processing tracks how later turns revise earlier propositions and whether a speaker is reporting a decision, floating an option or resisting an implication. The learning task uses an authored transcript until recorded audio is available; it cannot yet establish comprehension at natural speed or under real acoustic conditions.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Reconstruct the logic and rhetorical purpose of competing accounts. Test presuppositions, compression losses and shifts of agency. Preserve principled disagreement while producing a precise, audience-sensitive synthesis. Consider more than one defensible interpretation and explain why you prefer one.

Reading B
Decision ledger after the call: Review meeting—Monday. Launch—unconfirmed, dependent on accessibility testing. Supplier notice—draft now, send only after approval. The ledger must not turn preparation of a notice into permission to send it. No launch date was agreed.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Friday should work—sorry, Monday for the review, not the launch.
B: Assuming the access tests are complete?
A: For the launch, yes. We can review incomplete results on Monday.
B: Shall I notify the supplier?
A: Draft the notice, certainly, but hold it until we approve the next step.

Listening script B
Let me disentangle that. Monday is fixed for reviewing what we have; it is not a promise that testing will be complete. We may prepare the supplier message in parallel. Sending it remains a separate decision, and nothing in this call authorises launch.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prosody strategically to manage complex interaction, signal parenthetical qualification and distinguish reported from personal stance. Judge communicative effect and intelligibility, not resemblance to an idealised native accent.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
