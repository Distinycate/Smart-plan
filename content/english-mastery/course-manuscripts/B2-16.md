Reading A
A fictional college is considering a shared project space. Students want evening access, staff want clear responsibility for equipment, and the facilities team needs time for cleaning. A survey suggests interest, but it mainly reached students already involved in clubs. Our group prepares a briefing that separates common ground from unresolved issues. Everyone supports useful access; they disagree about hours, supervision and costs. We recommend a limited pilot with transparent booking rules and a review of who is excluded. The briefing includes a decision table rather than claiming that consultation produced complete agreement. A workable synthesis can clarify disagreement and next steps without pretending to settle every value conflict.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Map claim, supporting evidence, alternative explanation and limitation. Compare sources on the same question. Build a proportionate conclusion and address a fair counterargument. Explain why your evidence supports a claim rather than merely repeating it.

Reading B
Facilities note: Room available until 20:00 on weekdays if a named supervisor is present. Cleaning requires a thirty-minute gap before closure. Student proposal: access until 21:00 and self-managed booking. Funding for supervision after 20:00 has not been identified.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Can we say all groups support evening access?
B: Broadly, but their conditions differ.
A: What is the key unresolved issue?
B: Supervision and funding after eight, as well as time for cleaning.
A: We'll state the shared goal and keep those conditions visible.

Listening script B
Your final briefing should distinguish source statements, your evaluation and your recommendation. Attribute conflicting requirements accurately. Give the decision-maker a feasible next step and identify the evidence or authority needed before the proposal can become a commitment.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prominence to distinguish your position from a reported view. Pause at changes of argument. Signal concession and contrast with phrasing, without relying on exaggerated stress to make weak evidence sound stronger.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
