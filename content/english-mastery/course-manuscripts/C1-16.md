Reading A
A fictional partnership plans a shared learning centre. The funder wants a measurable outcome within six months; community representatives want access for people who rarely attend existing services; staff want a workload that can be sustained. These priorities overlap but are not interchangeable. A synthesis organised only by source would leave the decision-maker to connect them. Our briefing instead groups the material by access, evaluation and delivery capacity, attributing each claim and retaining conditions. It recommends a staged pilot, specifies what will be measured and explains which needs the measures might miss. The aim is an accountable decision, not a document that sounds consensual because all disagreement has been edited away.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Track stance, scope and evidential status across sources. Distinguish concession from endorsement and implication from explicit assertion. Synthesize by issue, retaining unresolved disagreement. Adapt density and register to the reader without changing commitments.

Reading B
Source notes: Funder—continuation depends on an agreed evaluation plan. Community group—attendance alone may miss barriers faced by non-users. Staff—capacity limited to three evenings weekly unless additional support is provided. No party has approved a permanent service or a universal success criterion.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Can we promise measurable impact in six months?
B: We can propose measures, but we must distinguish activity counts from longer-term impact.
A: What should the briefing say?
B: State what the pilot can test, what it cannot establish and whose approval is still required.

Listening script B
Present the shared purpose, then the material tensions. Attribute each condition and identify its decision owner. End with a sequence of actions that is feasible under the stated resources. Preserve uncertainty where resolving it would require evidence the sources do not provide.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Shape long turns into coherent rhetorical units. Use prominence and pauses to mark qualification, reformulation and source shifts. Intonation can suggest stance but is not conclusive evidence of intention by itself.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
