Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
The committee approved the pilot, provided that access arrangements were clarified before registration opened. Had the condition been omitted from the minutes, readers might reasonably have assumed that approval was unconditional. The sentence places the main decision first and its limiting condition afterwards; reversing that order would foreground the unresolved work. Although both versions can be accurate, they guide attention differently. Complex syntax is therefore a tool for organising relationships, not a display of length. A subordinate clause should make time, concession or dependency easier to follow. When several qualifications accumulate, dividing the sentence may preserve precision more effectively than adding another clause.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Track stance, scope and evidential status across sources. Distinguish concession from endorsement and implication from explicit assertion. Synthesize by issue, retaining unresolved disagreement. Adapt density and register to the reader without changing commitments.

Reading B
Editing comparison: A—Having reviewed the access plan, the pilot was approved. B—Having reviewed the access plan, the committee approved the pilot. Version A leaves the understood reviewer awkwardly attached to the pilot. Version B names the actor. Revision should clarify agency without inventing who actually performed the review.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Could I begin with the condition instead?
B: Yes: Provided that access is clarified, the committee approves the pilot.
A: Does that change the decision?
B: It need not, but it changes the emphasis. Check tense and whether the condition has already been met.

Listening script B
In a long sentence, identify the main clause and each dependent relationship. Check what every pronoun and reduced clause refers to. If the reader must hold too many unresolved details at once, divide the sentence while preserving the logical links.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Shape long turns into coherent rhetorical units. Use prominence and pauses to mark qualification, reformulation and source shifts. Intonation can suggest stance but is not conclusive evidence of intention by itself.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
