Environment & Community

Use the language of environment & community to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
Residents noticed litter near the community garden after weekend events. Some wanted more bins; others thought the problem was unclear signs. A volunteer group counted visible litter in two areas on three mornings. They found more items beside the entrance, but they could not tell who had left them. The group proposed a two-week trial with clearer signs and one extra bin. They planned to count again using the same method. Their small observation could guide a local trial, but it could not prove why people behaved as they did or represent every part of the town.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Identify the purpose, relevant constraints and sequence. Distinguish an observation from an explanation. Plan a connected response with reasons, an example and a clear next step. State what remains uncertain.

Reading B
Trial plan: Put one extra bin near the garden entrance. Add signs at both exits. Count visible litter at the same time on six mornings. Record event size and weather. Review the result before expanding the plan.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

Listening script B
Describe the observation before suggesting a cause. Compare like with like: the same area, time and method. If the number changes, consider other explanations such as event size or weather. Present the trial as a proposal, not a guaranteed solution.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use thought groups to make a connected account easy to follow. Stress contrasts and important dates. Repair a sentence calmly when needed; a useful repair is stronger than continuing with a misleading claim.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
