Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
The review distinguishes evidence that lends weight to an explanation from evidence that bears out a prediction. The first expression increases plausibility; the second suggests that an expected pattern has been observed. Neither automatically establishes causation. Elsewhere, a witness casts doubt on the timetable without calling the entire project into question. Treating these phrases as interchangeable erases the scope of the challenge. Phraseology is not ornamental vocabulary added after reasoning is complete. A recurrent combination can encode stance, distribute responsibility or signal how a claim relates to earlier discussion. Competent use therefore requires sensitivity to its usual company, its pragmatic force and the particular proposition it modifies.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Reconstruct the logic and rhetorical purpose of competing accounts. Test presuppositions, compression losses and shifts of agency. Preserve principled disagreement while producing a precise, audience-sensitive synthesis. Consider more than one defensible interpretation and explain why you prefer one.

Reading B
Editorial choices: The new figures lend weight to the staffing explanation. The audit bears out the prediction of a shortfall. One disputed date casts doubt on the timetable. It does not, by itself, undermine the rationale for the service. These sentences make different commitments.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

Listening script B
Build a lexical network around the function, not a list of supposed synonyms. Note which expression introduces corroboration, which expresses reservation and which signals a concession. Test each choice in a complete sentence and ask what a careful reader would infer about the strength and scope of the evidence.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prosody strategically to manage complex interaction, signal parenthetical qualification and distinguish reported from personal stance. Judge communicative effect and intelligibility, not resemblance to an idealised native accent.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.

Phraseology is not ornamental vocabulary added after reasoning is complete. A recurrent combination can encode stance, distribute responsibility or signal how a claim relates to earlier discussion. Competent use therefore requires sensitivity to its usual company, its pragmatic force and the particular proposition it modifies.
