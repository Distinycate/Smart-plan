Advanced Discourse & Rhetorical Analysis
เป้าหมาย: อธิบายว่าสถาปัตยกรรมข้อความจัดน้ำหนักเหตุผลและเสียงของผู้มีส่วนได้เสียอย่างไร

Use the language of advanced discourse & rhetorical analysis to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
A report opens with a vivid account of one satisfied participant, postpones aggregate results until the appendix and ends by repeating the participant's praise. This circular structure gives the anecdote interpretive priority even if the numerical findings are cautiously worded. Discourse analysis asks not only whether individual sentences are accurate, but how placement, proportion and attribution shape the reader's overall judgement. Moving the data forward would alter the argumentative architecture without changing any figure. A fair critique should also consider genre: an invitation and an evaluation report have different purposes. Genre explains a choice; it does not automatically excuse a misleading impression where readers reasonably expect evaluation.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Reconstruct the logic and rhetorical purpose of competing accounts. Test presuppositions, compression losses and shifts of agency. Preserve principled disagreement while producing a precise, audience-sensitive synthesis. Consider more than one defensible interpretation and explain why you prefer one.

Reading B
Report inventory: Opening story—one participant's positive account. Main section—three pages on delivery. Appendix—responses split evenly between positive and neutral; non-response rate unknown. Closing—opening praise repeated. No negative responses were recorded, but the report cannot establish that none existed among non-respondents.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

Listening script B
Trace the movement from opening frame to final recommendation. Identify whose voice is quoted directly, whose is compressed and whose is absent. Then distinguish the report's explicit claim from the inference encouraged by its architecture. Propose a revision that preserves legitimate purpose while improving the visibility of relevant limitations.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prosody strategically to manage complex interaction, signal parenthetical qualification and distinguish reported from personal stance. Judge communicative effect and intelligibility, not resemblance to an idealised native accent.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
