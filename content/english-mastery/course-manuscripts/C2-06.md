## Understand the situation

Cross-Source Synthesis & Reconstruction
เป้าหมาย: สังเคราะห์ข้ามแหล่งโดยรักษาความต่างของหน่วยวัด เวลา และกลุ่มข้อมูล

Use the language of cross-source synthesis & reconstruction to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
Three reports appear to disagree about a fictional reading programme. The first records increased registrations, the second unchanged weekly attendance and the third fewer visits per active member. These findings are not necessarily inconsistent: a larger registered population can coexist with stable attendance and less frequent use by each active participant. A synthesis that simply labels the evidence mixed conceals the difference between the denominators. Reconstruction requires identifying each source's population, period, unit of analysis and operational definition before arranging claims around a shared question. Where these cannot be aligned, the writer should retain the incompatibility rather than manufacture a common metric that none of the sources actually measured.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Reconstruct the logic and rhetorical purpose of competing accounts. Test presuppositions, compression losses and shifts of agency. Preserve principled disagreement while producing a precise, audience-sensitive synthesis. Consider more than one defensible interpretation and explain why you prefer one.

Reading B
Source ledger: A—registrations rose from 80 to 100 over one term. B—average weekly attendance remained 40 during that term. C—average visits per active member fell from four to three, but active membership was defined differently from registration. No source measures learning gains or explains causation.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Can I say participation improved?
B: Only after defining participation. Registrations increased; the other measures did not.
A: Should I average the figures?
B: No. They have different units and denominators. Present the distinction before drawing any overall judgement.

Listening script B
Create a claim map with a source beside every proposition. Mark comparable measures, unresolved definitions and genuinely contradictory statements separately. Organise the final synthesis around the decision the reader faces, but do not let a tidy structure conceal differences that would change that decision.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prosody strategically to manage complex interaction, signal parenthetical qualification and distinguish reported from personal stance. Judge communicative effect and intelligibility, not resemblance to an idealised native accent.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
