Reading A
My flatmate and I disagreed about noise in the evening. I thought he knew that I had an early shift, but I had never explained my new timetable. Instead of accusing him of being inconsiderate, I described the problem: I could hear music after ten and was finding it difficult to sleep. He explained that he practised guitar after work. Reading B
Shared agreement: Quiet hours from 22:00 to 07:00. Discuss exceptions in advance. Review the arrangement after one week. Either person can raise a concern. Agreement does not mean that both people had the same preference.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: Could we talk about the music after ten? I have an early shift.
B: I didn't realise your hours had changed. When could I practise?
A: Earlier in the evening would help.
B: Let's try that for a week and check again.

Listening script B
Use an example that the other person can recognise. Explain its effect on you, then invite their view. Avoid always and never unless they are accurate. A useful agreement says what will happen and when you will review it.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use thought groups to make a connected account easy to follow. Stress contrasts and important dates. Repair a sentence calmly when needed; a useful repair is stronger than continuing with a misleading claim.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
