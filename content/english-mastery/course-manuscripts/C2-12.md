## Understand the situation

Sophisticated Interaction & Negotiation

Use the language of sophisticated interaction & negotiation to complete the unit's practical or interpretive task.
Identify and relay the essential information in the two readings without adding unsupported facts.
Respond to the two listening scripts with appropriate clarification and source-sensitive wording.

Reading A
In a fictional venue negotiation, both sides say they can accept a later opening, but their assent rests on different conditions. The organiser assumes staffing costs will be covered; the venue assumes the organiser will supply staff. Apparent agreement conceals an unresolved allocation of responsibility. A skilled negotiator tests the reference of vague expressions such as that arrangement before treating assent as closure. They distinguish interests from positions, identify which terms can move together and record dependencies explicitly. Diplomatic language should protect the working relationship without making a conditional concession appear unconditional or allowing silence to be counted as consent.

Read for purpose, then locate the people, events, conditions and limits. Reconstruct the logic and rhetorical purpose of competing accounts. Test presuppositions, compression losses and shifts of agency. Preserve principled disagreement while producing a precise, audience-sensitive synthesis. Consider more than one defensible interpretation and explain why you prefer one.

Reading B
Provisional record: Opening may move to eight o'clock if staffing and costs are agreed. The organiser can supply two volunteers, subject to confirmation. The venue requires a named supervisor. Neither party has accepted the additional cost or authorised an announcement to participants.

Compare the two inputs: what is shared, what is additional, and what is not known? Use only details actually supplied. All characters, organisations, figures and events in these inputs are fictional classroom materials.

## Listen, clarify and interact

Listening script A — authored script; recorded audio and human audio QC are pending. Reading the transcript is text-based preparation, not evidence of independent listening ability.

A: We could live with that arrangement.
B: Before I record agreement, do you mean the later time only, or the staffing proposal as well?
A: The time only. Our staffing offer depends on two volunteers confirming.
B: Then let us record the time as conditional and keep cost allocation open.

Listening script B
Summarise each party's commitment in terms they can correct. Ask about the condition most likely to be interpreted differently. Close with owners and next steps rather than a vague declaration of consensus. If authority is limited, state whose further approval is required before anything becomes binding in this exercise.

First pass: identify purpose and main message. Second pass: note essential details and any corrections. Check against the script only after the listening attempt when approved audio becomes available. For now, practise with a teacher or partner reading the script.

Pronunciation and prosody
Use prosody strategically to manage complex interaction, signal parenthetical qualification and distinguish reported from personal stance. Judge communicative effect and intelligibility, not resemblance to an idealised native accent.

Interaction rehearsal: take both roles. Ask one clarification question, answer it using the source, and check that the other person has understood. Do not equate quietness, accent or hesitation with lack of ability.
