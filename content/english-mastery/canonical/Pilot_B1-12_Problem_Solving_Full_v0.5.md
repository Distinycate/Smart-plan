## Can-do outcomes
Explain a familiar problem; identify criteria; suggest alternatives; compare options; clarify; disagree cooperatively; reach a provisional decision; relay the plan.

# Lesson 1 — Define the problem and criteria
A group of **32 people** plans a one-day educational trip. The original bus booking was cancelled.

Constraints:
- decision by 5 p.m.;
- maximum transport budget **17,500 THB**;
- everyone arrives before 10 a.m.;
- group prefers to travel together;
- return transport after 5 p.m. must exist.

| Option | Cost | Time | Capacity / constraint |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bus | 450 THB/person | 2h30 | 18 seats only |
| Train | 520 THB/person | 2h10 | 40 seats; transfer needed |
| Minivan | 390 THB/person | 2h00 | two vehicles, **16 seats each**; quoted total covers all 32 passengers; limited luggage |

Reading tasks: infeasible alone? **Bus** · train total? **16,640 THB** · minivan passenger total? **12,480 THB** · non-price criteria? **capacity/time/togetherness/return/transfer/luggage**

# Lesson 2 — Suggest and compare
We could… / Why don’t we…? / Another option is… / It would be cheaper/faster/easier… / The advantage is… / The problem with that option is… / We need to check…

Model: The minivans are cheaper and a little faster. However, the group has to use two vehicles, and luggage space may be limited. The train is more expensive, but everyone can travel together.

Task: compare train and minivan using **three** criteria without deciding yet.

# Lesson 3 — Clarify and disagree
Do you mean…? / Could you explain that? / I see your point, but… / I’m not sure that will work because… / What if we…?

### Listening `AUD-B1-12-L03-001`
I prefer the train because everyone can travel together. It is more expensive than the minivans, and we still need to check the return timetable. The station transfer could also be difficult for some people. Before we decide, I think we should confirm the late return service.

Targets: preference **train** · benefit **together** · concerns **cost/transfer** · unresolved **return service** · action **confirm timetable**

# Lesson 4 — Reach a provisional decision
1. eliminate infeasible option;
2. compare at least two feasible plans;
3. state one uncertainty;
4. choose provisional plan;
5. assign next action.

Group A prioritises price. Group B prioritises travelling together. Group C prioritises simplest logistics. There is **no hidden correct answer**.

# Lesson 5 — Transfer scenario
Community workshop, group size **38**, budget **10,000 THB**.

| Venue | Cost | Capacity | Location | Limitation |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| A | 8,000 | 45 | city centre | no parking |
| B | 6,500 | 35 | near bus station | too small for 38 |
| C | 9,500 | 60 | outside town | transport needed |

Some participants do not drive. Compare, clarify, decide, state next step.

## Guided Practice
1. The problem ___ that the bus was cancelled. → **is**
2. We ___ take the train. → **could**
3. Train is ___ expensive than minivan. → **more**
4. Do you ___ the return train? → **mean**
5. I see your point, ___… → **but**
6. Infeasible whole-group option? → **bus**
7. Feasible by capacity? → **train/minivan**
8. Train uncertainty? → **return timetable/transfer**
9. “Best” answer without reasons gets full marks? → **No**
10. Can mediation invent agreement? → **No**

## Extended reception validation object
### Reading `TXT-B1-12-EXT-001` — The return-time problem
The group has almost agreed to take the train, but one question is still open. The outward train arrives early enough, and the total ticket cost is within budget. However, the organiser discovers that the direct return train leaves at 4:40 p.m., twenty minutes before the planned activity ends. A later train is available at 6:15, but it requires one change and arrives home much later. Some participants are happy to leave the activity early, while others think the group should keep the full programme and use the later train. A third possibility is to use the train in the morning and hire two minivans for the return journey, but the return price has not been confirmed. The group now needs to decide which information matters most and what must be checked before payment is made.

Tasks: identify the new problem; compare two realistic responses; state what information is still missing; explain which criterion your group would prioritise and why.

### Listening `AUD-B1-12-EXT-001`
> I think we are close to a decision, but I don’t want us to pay until we check the return journey. Leaving at 4:40 would be easy, but it means cutting the programme short. The 6:15 train lets us finish everything, although the change may be difficult with a large group. I’d like someone to call the minivan company as well. If the return price is reasonable, we could take the train there and the vans home. Then we can compare all three plans before five o’clock.

Listening targets: preferred process, advantages/disadvantages, missing information, proposed next action.

# v0.4 Targeted Revision — B1 validity fixes
## Hard constraints vs preferences
**Hard constraints:** 32 seats; total ≤17,500 THB; outward arrival before 10 a.m.; a usable return plan must be confirmed before payment.  
**Preferences:** travel together where possible; simpler transfer; more luggage space.

The late-return timetable is intentionally the **one required information gap** in the first decision cycle. Learners must recognise that payment should not be made until it is checked.

## Interaction scoring anchors
**4 Strong B1:** compares ≥2 feasible plans with reasons, responds to others, clarifies uncertainty, helps reach provisional outcome.  
**3 Generally B1:** contributes relevant options/reasons and responds, but development or clarification is uneven.  
**2 Developing:** gives a preference/reason but interaction is limited or misses an important constraint.  
**1 Limited:** isolated suggestions with little connection to criteria.  
**0:** no assessable contribution/off task.

Decision quality and interaction quality are recorded separately; choosing the assessor’s preferred option is never a criterion.

## Conflicting stakeholder input `TXT-B1-12-V04-STAKEHOLDERS`
The teacher wants the group to arrive together because attendance must be checked before the first activity. Two students prefer the minivans because they are cheaper and faster. Another student is worried about limited luggage space because the group needs presentation materials. The organiser is mainly concerned about confirming a safe return after 5 p.m. Nobody disagrees about the budget, but they give different priorities to convenience, cost, luggage and return certainty.

Task: identify each stakeholder priority and propose a question that would help the group decide.


### Additional reading object `TXT-B1-12-V05-002` — Return-service update
The train company confirms that the last return train leaves at 18:20 and arrives at 20:35. The group must reach the station by 18:05. A local shuttle can take 32 passengers from the activity site to the station, but it must be booked before noon.

Questions: latest safe arrival at station? **18:05** · new action required? **book shuttle before noon** · does this remove every train uncertainty? **No; transfer logistics still need planning.**
